# Kitchen tablet

On screens **960 pixels wide or more**, the board switches to a three-column kitchen layout: today, the coming days, and the next shop plus the freezer. A 10-inch tablet in landscape is wide enough; an 8-inch tablet shows the phone layout.

## Devices

| Device | Approx. price | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Amazon Fire HD 10 | €100–150 | Cheapest 10" screen, uses the Silk browser instead of Chrome |
| Samsung Galaxy Tab A9+ (11") | €170–230 | Regular Android with Chrome, easier setup |
| Lenovo Tab M11 | €150–200 | Similar to the Samsung |
| iPad (base model) | from approx. €380 | Smoothest; use Guided Access and set Auto-Lock to Never |

Echo Show or Nest Hub can't show a custom web page. E-ink screens aren't good for tapping, and OLED screens burn in on an always-on display.

## Full screen and keeping the screen on

On wide screens there's a **Vollbild** (full screen) button at the top right. It hides the browser bar and keeps the screen on. After standby you need to tap it again.

## Kiosk mode (Android / Fire)

For a permanent setup, use **Fully Kiosk Browser**:

1. Install the app from fully-kiosk.com (on a Fire tablet, download it in Silk and allow installs from that source).
2. Set the **Start URL** to your board address, e.g. `http://fedora.local:8080`. If you set a password, log in once.
3. Turn on **Keep Screen On** and **Reload on Screen On**.
4. Optional (Plus version): nightly screen-off, wake on motion, kiosk lock with PIN.

## Mounting (rental-friendly)

1. Use a **stand** for the first few weeks to find the right spot.
2. A **magnetic mount** on the side of a kitchen cabinet: no drilling, and the tablet lifts off.
3. An **under-cabinet swing arm**: keeps the counter clear, and there's often an outlet nearby.

Power it with an angled USB-C cable (approx. 2 m) in a cable channel. Keep it away from the stove, the sink and direct sun. Since the tablet charges all the time, a nightly sleep schedule is easier on the battery.
